Master's thesis, University of Bamberg

Emotion recognition in argumentative text

Studied how well models detect emotions in arguments, and how those emotions relate to how convincing an argument is.

Compared fine-tuned transformers (RoBERTa, DeBERTa) with instruction-tuned LLMs (Mistral, Zephyr) across zero-shot transfer, few-shot and chain-of-thought prompting, and TF-IDF-based example selection. Supervised by Lynn Greschner and Prof. Dr. Roman Klinger.

  • Supervised transfer breaks down across domains: adding even 95 in-domain examples per class recovers part of the loss.
  • Prompting strategy matters more than expected: few-shot helped Mistral (0.169) but hurt Zephyr (0.039), and chain-of-thought helped neither.
  • Emotions correlate weakly but systematically with convincingness: anger negatively, pride and relief positively.
0.52 → 0.15DeBERTa macro-F1 falls from in-domain emotion data to argumentative text, showing how large the domain shift is
